# Scientific salvage options

**Assessment: not currently defensible as a fully verified submission.** The framework is coherent, and several primary records survive, but RQ1 and timing are summary-only, the historical source-to-binary chain is incomplete, the prompt mitigation patch is malformed, and LM Studio persistence lacks the original log. This strategy retains novelty but leaves critical reviewer risks unresolved.

## Strategy B: narrow unsupported contributions

**Assessment: conditionally defensible after author-approved revision.** Keep the four-boundary framework, but present RQ2 arithmetic as retained descriptive forensic observations, RQ4 slot transfer and continuation as retained per-trial observations, RQ3 as configuration- and version-bounded, and remove or sharply qualify timing AUC and causal mitigation claims. This preserves the strongest evidence while reducing the central reproducibility burden. Submission would still require an author decision about whether E4 RQ1/timing claims remain central.

## Strategy C: separate archival observations from verified contributions

**Assessment: the most honest no-new-model strategy.** Use a primary contribution centered on the boundary framework, retained per-trial slot observations, and independently recomputed aggregate memory arithmetic. Place GGUF, LM Studio logging, sanitisation causality, and timing AUC in an archival/exploratory evidence section with explicit grades. This is scientifically coherent, but novelty and impact are narrower and IEEE T-IFS reviewers may still require a stronger primary experimental chain.

## Recommendation

Strategy C, or a strict version of Strategy B, is defensible without new model weights. No strategy supports a claim that the complete original four-boundary study is fully reproducible or submission-ready. Minimum remaining work is author approval of claim scope, recovery of a valid historical implementation or explicit archival framing, and recovery of TTFT vectors if timing remains central.
